She has a word for everyone. She has none for him.

An excerpt · Chapter Sixteen

Tessa notices everything and says everything, and keeps every true thing behind the talk. Jack is the one man in Marblemount she cannot read. A week into something neither of them will name, he sets out to take the one thing she never gives.

It had stopped being a play, and that was the part that should have frightened him more than it did.

A week into it, he could no longer locate the moment management had become something else. There had been a version of this, early, that he could have defended to Wade with a straight face: stay close to the threat, keep her where you can read her, a chief doing the hard necessary thing. He had told himself that version for about four days. Then one night she had fallen asleep against him mid-sentence, in the middle of an argument about a book, her clever mouth finally still and her hand open on his chest, and he had lain awake under the weight of her and understood that there was no version left in which he was managing anything. He was just a man who drove up a dark road every night because the woman at the end of it had become the place he lived.
